The Little Black Book on Leadership

Leadership field glossary

Plain-language definitions for the CPA case exercises.

Return to the course

After-action review
A short debrief of what was supposed to happen, what actually happened, and what the team will change next time. Use one observed fact and assign one next action.
Affirmation
Specific recognition of a useful action and its effect. “Your timestamped handoff let Jamie review the file before the deadline” gives the behavior a name and explains why it mattered.
Constant pressure
Calm, predictable reinforcement of an agreed standard through small follow-ups. It should leave people able to raise problems early.
Correction
Private, timely guidance that names a gap between a known standard and observed work, checks the context, and sets an agreed follow-up.
Culture carrier
Someone others actually follow. A senior associate who always asks juniors for evidence can make a review standard routine across shifts or teams.
Document the truth
Record observable events, dates, agreed expectations, support and follow-up. Distinguish a fact from an inference about someone’s motives.
Ethos
The convictions that govern a leader’s decisions when following them costs time, convenience or popularity.
Hills you die on
A small set of clearly defined non-negotiable standards. Explain what a team member should do when a deadline or a powerful person challenges one.
Informal leader
A colleague whose behavior others copy, whether or not that person holds a formal title. Watch who people turn to when a deadline slips.
One-on-one
A direct individual conversation to understand a person’s goals, discuss specific behavior, and agree on one next action.
Ownership
Taking responsibility for the part of a result you can change, such as the clarity of a handoff or the training a colleague received.
Set the stage
Arrange the physical or digital work environment so the next correct action is visible, practical, and repeatable.
80% affirmation / 20% correction
The author’s reminder to notice and reinforce good work frequently. Immediate risks and material errors still require prompt intervention. Treat the ratio as a coaching cue rather than a performance target.
