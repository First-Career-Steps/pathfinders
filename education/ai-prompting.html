Exercise 1 of 3

Build a prompt with RTCF

A language model predicts what text plausibly comes next. That makes it useful for drafting and reformatting, but a fluent answer can still invent a fact. Give it a role, task, context, and format (RTCF). Include the relevant source again if a long conversation has drifted.

Worked example

“Tell me about databases” could produce a textbook chapter. Try “Act as a patient technical trainer. Explain when a spreadsheet stops being enough. Our store leads share one inventory sheet and sometimes overwrite counts. Give two examples and a three-row comparison.”

Your turn

Rewrite the weak database prompt, or use a task from your workplace. Build all four parts, then copy your prompt into an AI tool to compare results.

Exercise 2 of 3

Refine the first answer

Change one variable at a time. First add a constraint. Then change the reader or format. If tone matters, give a short example of the style you want.

Worked example

First pass: “A database improves consistency.” Follow-up 1: “Use our inventory example and name one tradeoff.” Follow-up 2: “Rewrite that as a 60-word note to an opening manager.” Now you can see which change helped.

Your turn

Run two follow-ups against the same answer in your AI tool. You can also revise the worked example by hand.

Exercise 3 of 3

Catch an unsupported claim

Trace an important claim to an independent source. Another model repeating it is weak evidence. Check the exact dates, limits, and exceptions before using the answer.

Worked example

Practice source: “Each store submits its count by 4 p.m. Friday. The regional lead reviews differences Monday.” AI draft: “Stores must submit by noon Friday for the regional lead’s same-day review.” Both the deadline and the review day changed.

Your turn

Audit the example against its source, or verify a factual answer from your own field.
