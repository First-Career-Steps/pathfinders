Exercise 1 of 3
Open up the options
Separate generating from judging. Start with quantity. Ask for 15 unconventional ideas, group them into themes, then expand your most interesting three before ranking them.
Worked example
A shift handoff keeps missing delivery changes. Three different directions are a spoken read-back, a shared change log, and a reminder tied to the delivery time. Each tackles a different point of failure.
Your turn
Pick something you use every day. Use Quantity first in the library to generate ideas, or list them yourself. Keep three genuinely different directions.

Exercise 2 of 3
Change the lens
SCAMPER asks what you could Substitute, Combine, Adapt, Modify, Put to another use, Eliminate, or Reverse. Then look through the eyes of a skeptical buyer, a new user, or a competitor. Constraints often expose a simpler idea.
Worked example
Reverse the handoff: the incoming lead asks for outstanding changes. A skeptical operator asks who will remember. A constraint of five minutes and no new software suggests testing the existing shift sheet first.
Your turn
Apply one SCAMPER move. Compare its value and effort with another idea and choose a direction you could test within two weeks.

Exercise 3 of 3
Design the smallest useful test
An experiment should tell you whether an assumption holds. Define who tries it, what you observe, and the result that would change your decision.
Worked example
Assumption: incoming leads will use the change log during a rush. Try it with two shifts this week. Count how many delivery changes are read back correctly. If both shifts skip the log, change the handoff routine before building an app.
Your turn
Stress-test the winning idea. Decide what you need to learn before spending money.
