The remake that lost its placeRestaurant Leadership

A remake lands beside two pizzas for different delivery orders. Four names are being called at pickup. The cashier asks for an ETA while a cook starts another copy of the remake.

What do you do in the next thirty seconds?
Take the remake yourself.

You can now track the remake. The other two pizzas have reached the cut table, and nobody knows who is reconciling the tickets.

The cook asks which pizza belongs to the driver. What do you do?
Keep making pizzas and tell the driver whichever box comes out first is probably theirs.

The remake moves faster, but the driver would leave without a confirmed handoff.

Assign the expediter to match tickets and call the next two orders; have the cashier relay the verified ETA.

Two people now have distinct jobs. Matching tickets lets the expediter recover the sequence while you finish the remake.

Give one expediter the ticket rail and ask the cashier to acknowledge the wait.

The line stops duplicating orders. The expediter finds an additional pizza nobody can match. A guest asks for an exact time.

What information can the cashier give now?
Identify the guest’s order at the rail, tell them its actual stage, and promise a specific time for the next update.

A guest can make a decision with a verified status and a time to hear more. The expediter retains control of the sequence.

Quote the normal pickup time from the screen while the expediter sorts out the extra pizza.

A standard quote ignores the mismatch and may leave the cashier making a second promise they cannot keep.

Go to the lobby and talk with waiting guests.

The lobby gets quieter. At the line, two people still call tickets at once. A second driver asks whether to take an unlabelled box.

How do you keep the lobby informed while recovering the line?
Stay in the lobby and ask each cook to call their own order louder.

Multiple callers still control the same queue. More volume makes the sequence harder to hear.

Ask the cashier to handle lobby updates and name one person to verify each box against its ticket.

You can return to the bottleneck while guests keep a point of contact. Verified handoffs prevent another remake.

The missing supportLeading the Financial Close

At 2:10 p.m. Tuesday the controller wants an internal flash by 3:30. This team’s release checklist requires identified support and a second reader before an item may be marked reviewed. A $184,000 vendor batch includes $61,000 without receiving confirmations. Maya can obtain confirmations by 4:15. Ben can review the supported items by 3:05. Yesterday the CFO said an explicitly provisional flash was acceptable. The operations lead now asks you to mark the whole batch green because the difference usually reverses.

Which work can you mark ready for review now?
The identified $123,000 with its supporting documents

Its evidence is available and its review remains visible.

The full $184,000 with a reminder

A reminder cannot establish receipt for the missing portion.

None of the batch until 4:15

Holding supported work consumes scarce reviewer time.

Five review notes for one analystCoaching Accounting Teams

Quinn’s last three account reconciliations drew five review notes. Two concerned an obsolete template the whole team still uses. Two missed items occurred after a file export changed. One was an overdue escalation despite a written deadline. Quinn completed the old export reliably. The supervisor never demonstrated the new export. The reviewer suggests putting Quinn on a general improvement plan. Quinn asks which specific part of the work to practice.

Which pair most directly supports a team-level fix?
The two changed-export notes.

A changed method may require teaching before attributing cause.

The two obsolete-template notes.

The shared tool creates exposure for everyone using it.

All five notes together.

Combining categories conceals the different actions they need.

Three branches, three rejection ratesChanging Finance Operations

Three regional teams use the same invoice intake policy. North had 18 returns out of 180 submissions, South had 9 out of 45, and East had 8 out of 160. South recently switched to a portal with a hidden reference field. North has more new staff. Four South returns and two North returns were resubmissions of the same invoices. The director wants to rank supervisors by the raw number of returns.

Which region has the highest return-event rate per submission?
North.

18 is the largest count but its volume is four times South’s.

East.

8 divided by 160 is 5%.

South.

9 divided by 45 is 20%, compared with 10% and 5%.
